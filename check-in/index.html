Client mode

    The session brief is a short plain-text summary, handy to paste into your notes or bring to a 1 to 1 session. Nothing from this check-in is kept. Close the tab and it’s gone.

    How it works.

    1. Prime. Start with one thing that went right today.
    2. Scale. Rate your headspace from 0 to 10, name what keeps it from being a point lower, and what would feel good enough today.
    3. Reset. Three to five rounds of cyclic sighing on the orb: two breaths in, one long breath out.
    4. Clarity. Picture tomorrow going a notch better, and spot the first small sign of it.
    5. Step. Choose what would move you half a point, tie it to a daily cue, and check how doable it feels. Or skip it, and notice one small thing worth keeping before tomorrow.

    There’s no score at the end to pass or fail. You rate how doable your step feels, and if it’s under 7 you make it smaller until it isn’t.

    Why it looks forward

    It’s built on solution-focused ideas. Instead of digging into problems, it looks at what’s already working, what a slightly better tomorrow would look like, and the smallest step towards it. Small and specific tends to beat big and vague.

    The gears

    The orb is for your breathing: hold it on the way in, let go on the way out. The two gears are a rough picture of two parts of your brain: the part that thinks things through, and the part that raises the alarm. Under pressure, the alarm tends to run on its own. As you settle, the arm swings the small gear in until the two mesh and turn together. They move with what you tell the check-in. They don’t measure anything.

    What it isn’t

    This is a self-help tool for adults. It isn’t therapy, medical advice or a diagnosis, and it doesn’t replace your GP or a mental health professional. If you are in crisis, please use the .

    Your privacy

    There is no account, no cookies, no tracking and no saving. What you write never leaves this page, and it’s gone when you close the tab. The page is locked so it can’t send anything over the internet.

    You can add it to your home screen. After that it opens like an app and works with no signal at all, because your phone keeps its own copy of the page. Still nothing is saved or sent.

    If you arrive from Find your direction, a few of the ideas lean the way you chose and the gears move a little differently. That’s the only difference.

    If you work with Bluzen 1 to 1, you may be given a client link. Only that link offers to save check-ins, in this browser only, and only when you choose to.
